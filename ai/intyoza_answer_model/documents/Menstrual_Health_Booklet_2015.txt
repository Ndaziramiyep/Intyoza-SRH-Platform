What is menstruation?
Menstruation is a signal that, as a girl, you are growing up normally. Your body begins to develop and change as you transition from being a child to an adult, preparing so that you can have a baby someday. Your period is the 2 to 7 days that a girl experiences menstrual flow, meaning blood and tissues leave your body through your vagina. It is important to remember that all girls go through this change. Menstruation is a sign of normal growth and development. In other words, you are healthy and you are growing up just the way you should.

The menstrual cycle: what is going on inside your body?
You have two ovaries which make eggs so you can have a baby. One of your ovaries releases an egg to the uterus through the fallopian tubes. This is called ovulation. When the egg reaches your uterus, the lining of the uterus thickens. About 5 days after ovulation, the lining thickens further. If the egg is fertilized by a male's sperm, it leads to pregnancy. If the egg is not fertilized, the lining comes off the walls of the uterus after about 14 days and leaves your body as blood. After the menstrual period, the cycle begins again.

When do girls get their first period?
You can begin menstruating any time from age 8 to 18, although most girls start having their period between the ages of 10 and 14.

How often will you get your period?
It normally happens every 28 days, but it can be anywhere from 21 to 35 days. You may have irregular periods for the first year or two as your body adjusts to many changes. You may also have irregular periods if you are under stress, exercising heavily, eating poorly, or in an emergency situation.

How long will your period last?
Your bleeding can last for 2 to 7 days. It can vary, and it may be different for many girls.

Is there a bad smell when you have your period?
The bad smell that you sometimes notice comes from bacteria that begin to grow when it is warm and moist. Changing your pad or cloth often, every 3 to 5 hours or when the cloth or pad becomes soaked, will help remove the smell and reduce the chance of getting an infection.

Is it normal to feel pain during your period?
Yes. You may feel pain in your lower back or in your stomach when you have your period. To relieve the discomfort it helps to move and stretch, do some exercise, put a hot compress on the painful area, or take a warm bath. If available, pain medicine such as ibuprofen may help relieve the pain.

Why do you have a clear or white discharge during the month?
When you are not menstruating, you may have vaginal discharge. This is normal. Discharge may be clear, cloudy white or yellowish. Changes in discharge may be a sign of infection.

What can you do to stay clean and healthy during your period?
Be prepared. Use sanitary pads or clean cloth pads to absorb the blood. Change your sanitary pad or cloth pad every 3 to 5 hours, or more often if needed. Keep a sanitary pad or cloth pad with you at all times, even if you do not think your period is coming, especially when you are at school. Take a bath as needed and wash the genital area with soap and water at least twice a day. Wash your hands with soap and water after using the toilet or changing pads. To wash your hands: wet your hands, use soap, lather, scrub, rinse, and shake your hands to dry.
Wipe your private area from front to back after using the toilet to prevent infection. Do not use special products, perfumes, herbs or ointments on your private parts. Watch out for signs of infection: changes in discharge, bad smell, itching and a burning feeling.

How can you use a calendar to track your period?
Use a calendar to note the day your period starts, how long it lasts, and how heavy it is on each day. Your cycle will probably be about the same length each month and have a similar heaviness of flow on the same days of your cycle, so you can use the calendar to predict when your next period will begin and what it will be like. From the end of each period, count 21 to 35 days to predict the day of your next period. It is usually every 28 days, but some girls have it earlier or later. Mark the days you have your period by circling them.

What if blood leaks through your clothing?
It happens to almost every girl at some point. Sometimes it happens when you are not at home, so you cannot change right away. If you are wearing a sweater or jacket, you can take it off and tie it around your waist. Then get a new pad so that it does not bleed through your clothes more than it already has. To avoid this, change your pads regularly and keep extras in your bag.

How do you dispose of and care for menstrual materials?
Wrap used sanitary pads in paper or plastic. Throw used sanitary pads in a rubbish bin. Never throw sanitary pads in the toilet. Use only clean and dry cloth pads. Wash cloth pads with clean water and soap. Dry cloth pads by hanging them in the sun, because the sun helps kill germs that may cause infection.

Menstrual myths: true or false?
You can take a bath when you have your period: TRUE. Bathing and washing are good habits to keep clean during your period. Being in water will not cause sickness or madness and may help prevent infection during menstruation.
Eating sour or salty foods can stop your period: FALSE. Eating sour or salty foods will not stop your menstrual flow. Neither will drinking cold drinks. You should eat whatever makes you feel good during menstruation, including healthy choices.
You should not run or move around too much when you have your period: FALSE. You do not need to reduce your movement when you have your period. More physical activity may actually reduce pain from menstrual cramps and give you more energy. But if you have cramps and feel like limiting activity or resting more, listen to your body's needs.
Menstrual blood is dirty: FALSE. Menstruation is not a disease or an impurity. It is a natural biological experience of women and girls.

Who can you talk to?
It is okay to want to know more and to ask for help. Your questions and concerns should be shared. Talk to a trusted female teacher, friend, family member or nurse for help.
